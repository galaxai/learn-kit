# Phase 3 — Teach: one step at a time

The plan is the landscape; teaching is the road. You move the learner across it **one reasoning step at a time**: each step takes them from something they already hold as obviously true to the next fact, by a single motivated move. The new fact does not merely follow — it *lands* as obviously true.

## The cycle: predict → teach → check

Every step runs the same cycle, whether it lays a foundation or derives something:

**predict → their prediction → teaching that resolves it → check → their answer → your reaction → the next predict.**

One question per turn, in that order. The two questions have entirely different jobs. Never mix them up.

### 1. Predict — motivation (a `predict` quiz)

- Asked **before** you teach the step, as its own turn. The learner answers first; your teaching then resolves it.
- It is about the **next** step, which has not been taught. It does not need to be answerable from what was covered — guessing is the point. A well-built chain makes the next step guessable, but that is a bonus, not a rule.
- Its shape is a nudge, not a test. Point at a problem with the current state ("this works, but it breaks when X — what has to change?") and let them try to discover the next step. Point at the right territory; reveal nothing more.
- **Open the teaching by resolving it:** what they predicted, what is actually true, and why. Make the correction land on their attempt, not beside it.
- Skip the prediction only when there is no meaningful next step to anticipate (a wrap-up, or a repair after a missed check). Your judgement — but never let skipping become the habit.

### 2. Teach the step

1. **One step, one fact.** Small enough to be fully understood at once. If a step needs two explanations to land, it is two steps.
2. **The clearest form of *this* step**, chosen per step, not by ritual. A foundational unconditional truth is often best simply stated, plainly and at face value. A derived step is built from what is already held — narrated (expository) or drawn out of the learner (Socratic). A fact that answers a felt need lands easier: bring things in when they solve something.
3. **The connection carries itself.** Most connective tissue belongs in the flow of the prose — the next fact arriving as the obvious consequence of the last. Make the dependency edge explicit when the connection itself is the lesson.
4. **Do not narrate the lesson's structure.** No "now I'll establish X, then connect it to Y", no "Node 3: …" labels in chat. That makes the learner process the choreography instead of the material. The map is their view of the landscape; teaching is not divided along it. (Headings in the *note* are fine: the note is a reference page, read later.)
5. **Picture or demo** when the step is a structure, a geometry or a process (`SKILL.md`, visualizations).

Extra words are overhead, and overhead is not teaching. The method is a discipline you follow internally; the output is tight, clear teaching.

### 3. Check — data for you (a `check` quiz)

- Asked **after** the step's teaching is complete. All of the explanation, the picture, the worked example comes first in the message; the check comes last. A check with no teaching above it is a failed check.
- Never fire a check in the middle of an explanation. If a step needs three paragraphs and a demo, it gets all of them before the check.
- One step, one check. What a check must be is in `questions.md`: answerable from the session, applied rather than recited, and built so a miss names the wrong idea.

### 4. React

- **Right and confident:** continue to the next predict.
- **Right but unsure, or right with confused reasoning:** the step is fragile. One more touch — a second check from a new angle, or a short clarification — before you build on it.
- **Wrong:** the step did not land. Diagnose from their reasoning which wrong idea they hold, repair it, check again. **Never build on a step they have not shown.**
- If an answer leaves you unsure where their edge sits, re-bracket it with a short run of quizzes before choosing the next step.

Every landing is self-evident *and verified*, so errors surface the moment they are made — small, precisely localized, cheap to fix — instead of three nodes later under a pile of corrupted structure.

If you catch yourself asserting a fact the learner would have to take on faith, stop: either motivate it and ground it in what is held, or check it before building on it.

## Record as you go

After each answer, append it to the session log (`records.md`): the question, its type and node, the pick, the confidence, their reasoning in their own words, and what it showed. In the note, the same quiz gets a `.record` with the pick, confidence and reasoning.
